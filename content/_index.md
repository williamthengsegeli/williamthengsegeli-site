My interests lie within the fields of political knowledge, voting patterns, and the intersection of technology with political attitudes. Methodologically, I utilize quantitative data to draw inferences from large sets of observations, while also incorporating experimental designs for causal inquiry. In particular, I find recent developments and possibilities in computational social science methods — including large language models (LLMs), machine learning algorithms, and text-as-data approaches — to be promising novel avenues for political science research. Consequently, a substantial part of my work attempts to leverage these large, novel datasets to better understand the political world.

Before enrolling in the graduate program at Georgia State University, I completed my bachelor's degree in political science at the University of Stavanger (UiS) in Norway. My bachelor's thesis, co-authored with Aud Nævdal and supervised by Associate Professor Carlo Michael Knotz, investigated the intersection of political knowledge and vote congruence in Norway. My time at UiS also allowed me to connect with Professor Tevfik Murat Yildirim, who provided crucial guidance and with whom I currently have the pleasure of co-authoring two upcoming articles.

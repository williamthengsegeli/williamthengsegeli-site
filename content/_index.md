My interests lie especially within the fields of political knowledge, voting patterns, and the intersection of technology with political attitudes. I use quantitative data to infer from large sets of observations, while also incorporating experimental designs for causal inquiries. In particular, I find recent developments within computational social science methods — LLMs, machine learning algorithms, and text-as-data — to be promising novel avenues for political science research. A substantial part of my work therefore attempts to take advantage of new, large sets of observations to learn more about our political world.

Before enrolling in the PhD program at Georgia State University, I completed my bachelor's degree in political science at the University of Stavanger in Norway. My bachelor thesis, co-authored with Aud Nævdal and supervised by Associate Professor Carlo Michael Knotz, investigated political knowledge and vote congruence in Norway. My years at UiS also allowed me to connect with Professor Tevfik Murat Yildirim, whom I have both learned a great deal from and currently have the pleasure of co-authoring a paper on media coverage of parliamentary representatives in a comparative context.
